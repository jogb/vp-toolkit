# Scoring & assembly model (authoritative)

This is the shared scoring vocabulary for both the vp-development and vp-diagnosis skills. An idea
scored here can be moved between the two skills without translation. Everything is judged **from the
target customer's perspective**, never the owner's.

The scale is **0–2 and deliberately harsh.** No half-points. No stretching to 0–5. Anything that
doesn't clearly meet the "2" description gets a 0 or 1.

> **This model has three axes — Benefit, Difference, Believability. There is no Clarity axis, on
> purpose.** Adding one would break the "three 8s" benchmark and flatten the dual Difference score.
> Clarity is handled only in the `vp-diagnosis` skill, as a *separate* page-expression check
> (🟢/🟡/🔴) that drives copy rewrites and is **never** multiplied into the score below.

---

## The three axes

### 1. Benefit — how much the target customer wants it
| Score | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **0** | They don't really care. If they wouldn't even have a conversation about it, it's a 0. |
| **1** | They care enough to have a conversation about it; they might even raise it themselves. |
| **2** | They care so much that hearing it makes them want to learn more. They'd jump through hoops to get it. It's practically a direct answer to something they've repeatedly hoped for. |

### 2. Difference — how unusual the target customer finds it (score TWICE)
Score every idea **twice**: once against **non-direct ("average") competitors**, once against
**direct competitors**. An idea can be unique in general yet ordinary against your closest rival — and
the direct score is what ultimately ranks it.
| Score | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **0** | They think it's common; not at all exclusive to you. |
| **1** | They think it's *almost* exclusive to you and/or clearly different from what competitors do. |
| **2** | They think it's exclusive to you and/or totally different from what competitors do. |

> If you have many direct competitors and effectively only one difference score, you'll produce one
> final product per idea instead of two. Otherwise, always keep the two difference scores separate.

### 3. Believability — how easily the target customer believes it
| Score | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **0** | They won't really believe it (even if they don't think you're outright lying). |
| **1** | They might believe it, but won't be instantly convinced. |
| **2** | They'll believe it without questions. |

---

## Improving a score — the specificity lever
The most common and most effective way to raise any score is to make the idea **more specific**:
- more specific about **what the customer wants** → raises Benefit
- more specific about **why it's a benefit** → raises Benefit
- more specific so it's **more exclusive** → raises Difference
- more specific so it's **easier to prove** → raises Believability

Naming the opposite often strengthens a benefit (e.g. "generic advice almost never creates permanent
change because it ignores your body, emotions, and life"). Combining two overlapping ideas into one
sharper idea often lifts both. You may change wording freely **as long as it stays honest.**

---

## Proof types (for Believability)
1. **Your words.** Weakest alone — people assume you're not telling the whole truth. Be relatable,
   focus on the customer not yourself, and be specific ("98% of our 4,522 clients…" > "most clients").
2. **Other people's words.** Testimonials, reviews, endorsements, referrals, trust seals/badges,
   association memberships. Often the most convincing. Good testimonial structure:
   **[my problem] → [how it felt] → [what solved it] → [how I feel now].**
3. **Studies & facts.** People trust studies and simple verifiable facts about the business (number
   of clients, articles, staff, prices).
4. **Give results in advance.** Trials, freebies, samples, useful articles — a taste of the outcome
   proves you can deliver the whole outcome.
5. **"Intuitively true."** A chain of arguments so self-evidently true the customer keeps nodding and
   never stops to doubt. Relies on argumentation skill; if one link is questionable the illusion
   breaks. If you can't make it intuitively true in a few sentences, use another proof type instead.

**No contradictions.** If anything on the page seems contradictory, unfocused, or incoherent, people
doubt *everything*. This is why a VP carries only a few, tightly-focused ideas.

## Assembly — turning scored ideas into a VP

### Filter before proving/assembling
1. Drop ideas that **don't score ≥1 vs direct competitors.**
2. Drop ideas that score **1 vs direct** but **not 2 vs non-direct.**
3. If fewer than three ideas survive, keep the **1 & 1** ideas.

### Compute two products per surviving idea
- **Non-direct score** = Benefit × Difference(non-direct) × Believability
- **Direct score** = Benefit × Difference(direct) × Believability

Each product's maximum is **8** (2 × 2 × 2). A single **0 on any axis zeroes the idea** — which is
exactly why you diagnose/improve the *lowest* axis, never an average.

### Rank
1. Order by the **direct** product (highest first).
2. Tiebreak with the **non-direct** product.
3. Then Benefit, then Difference(direct), then Believability.
4. Then judgment (how important a reason to choose you the idea really is).

### Select
- **Target three ideas that score 8** (ideally 8 & 8). You can't build a good VP on one idea.
- Two very strong ideas can work. Four is complicated (only if the fourth is really important). Five
  is almost impossible to communicate. Six: don't.
- **If no idea reaches 8, that is the finding.** The ideas aren't strong enough yet — loop back and
  improve specificity (Benefit/Difference) rather than shipping weak ideas.

### Three formats (owner's preference — doesn't change how the VP is used)
- **Story** — a short paragraph per idea, proof woven in. (Weak fit if your proof is mostly
  testimonials.)
- **Ideas + evidence** — a list of the ideas and a separate list of their proof. Simplest.
- **Story + separate evidence** — a short story of the ideas, with proof kept in a separate list.
  Often marginally best: it forces the ideas to mesh without cramming proof into the prose.

The finished VP is a **strategic checklist** of the ideas the marketing must convey — not ad copy,
not something pasted verbatim onto the page. Accuracy of the ideas beats word count and polish.
