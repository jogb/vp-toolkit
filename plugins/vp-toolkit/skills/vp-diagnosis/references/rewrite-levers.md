# Rewrite levers (copy-level only)

Every improvement this skill proposes is a **copy rewrite hypothesis** — headline, subhead, proof
line, or CTA — tied to the axis it's meant to lift and conditioned on the stated assumptions. Never
propose layout/UX changes as the deliverable; structural or visual issues may be *named* as
observations, but the fix stays copy. Every rewrite must stay **honest** — never invent a benefit,
proof, differentiator, or number the business hasn't confirmed. Where a rewrite needs a fact the page
doesn't supply (a real statistic, a real testimonial), write it as a **placeholder the owner fills**
(e.g. "[X]% of [N] clients…"), not a fabricated figure.

---

## The master lever: specificity
The single most effective copy fix for a weak axis is to make the claim **more specific**:
- more specific about **what the customer wants** → lifts Benefit
- more specific about **why it's a benefit** (often by naming the opposite) → lifts Benefit
- more specific about **what exactly is different** → lifts Difference
- more specific about **the verifiable detail** → lifts Believability

Combining two vague on-page ideas into one sharp idea frequently lifts multiple axes at once.

---

## Axis → rewrite move

| Weak axis | What the page is doing wrong | Copy move |
|---|---|---|
| **Benefit** | Stating a feature/fact, not the outcome the customer wants | Rewrite as the outcome in the customer's language; name the pain it removes or the hope it answers |
| **Difference** | Saying something every competitor also says ("great service", "quality") | Replace with the specific, hard-to-copy thing only this business does; if nothing on the page qualifies, this is a development gap → route to vp-development |
| **Clarity/Expression** *(page-diagnosis layer only)* | A strong idea is buried, abstract, jargon-laden, or below the fold | Surface it in the headline/subhead; say it plainly at a glance; cut the wind-up |
| **Believability** | An asserted claim with no proof, or a claim too big to swallow | Attach a proof type (below); or soften the claim to what the proof supports |

---

## Proof types (to lift Believability)
1. **Your words** — weakest alone; be specific and customer-focused.
2. **Other people's words** — testimonials, reviews, endorsements, trust seals, memberships. Strong.
   Testimonial structure: **[problem] → [how it felt] → [what solved it] → [how they feel now].**
3. **Studies & facts** — verifiable numbers about the business (clients, articles, staff, prices).
4. **Results in advance** — trial, freebie, sample, useful article.
5. **"Intuitively true"** — a short chain of self-evident statements the reader can't help but accept.

Specificity beats vagueness every time: "98% of our 4,522 clients would recommend us" >
"most clients are happy."

## Headline / subhead principles (for the lead idea)
A page's headline should carry the **highest-scoring idea**. Strong headlines do three things:
1. **Capture attention** — personal, emotionally live, concrete (not a fact everyone already knows).
2. **Create fascination** — a pull that makes the reader want to continue.
3. **Create anticipation** — an implied reward for reading on.

To fill any headline pattern, use the target customer's biggest **problems, fears, hopes, values,
beliefs** — in their words. A subhead should reinforce and extend the headline, not repeat it. (A
larger library of headline patterns exists in project knowledge; use it for inspiration, not as a
template to fill blindly.)

---

## Every improvement is a testable hypothesis
Frame each rewrite so it can be A/B tested, not asserted as fact:

```
Hypothesis: Rewriting [element] from "[current]" to "[proposed]"
will lift [axis] from [n] to [n], because [reason grounded in the target customer].
Assumes: [the specific assumption(s) this rests on].
Test: [headline test / one-idea landing page / proof-block variant].
Expected score lift: [old product → new product].
```

Prioritise hypotheses by **expected multiplicative score lift** — the biggest jump in an idea's
Benefit × Difference(direct) × Believability product comes first. Fixing a **0** on any single axis
(which currently zeroes the whole idea) almost always outranks improving an idea that's already at 4+.
